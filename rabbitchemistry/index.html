Scientific standardization notes

  • Hydration: Chemical formula weights differ between anhydrous and hydrated compounds. For example, copper(II) sulfate is 159.61 g/mol, whereas its pentahydrate is 249.68 g/mol.
  • Solutions: An aqueous compound retains the solute's formula weight, but the total solution has no single molar mass.
  • Mixtures: Coal, gunpowder, universal indicator, and litmus do not have one fixed molecular formula or molar mass.
  • Naming: Generic names such as iron nitrate, copper carbonate, and sodium phosphate may describe more than one compound. The tables use specified common forms, but the actual reagent should be identified before assigning a definitive formula.
  • Physical form: Powder, ribbon, lump, liquid, and gas classifications describe the material's form, not a different chemical formula. Some items, such as ice and mercury, are retained under the original headings even where their physical form differs from the heading.
